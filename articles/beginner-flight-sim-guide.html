Beginner Flight Sim Hardware Guide

Flight simulation allows non-pilots and aviation enthusiasts to experience flight. Flight simulations work with just a keyboard, but the immersion increases drastically with flight hardware, like a throttle (speed control) or yoke (steering wheel).

Choosing a Simulator

Many flight simulation software options exist, ranging in accuracy, price, and hardware requirements. Options include Microsoft Flight Simulator (MSFS), X-Plane, DCS World, and GeoFS.

Flight Hardware

Yokes & Joysticks

The primary flight control used to steer the aircraft. A yoke is common in airliners and general aviation aircraft, while a joystick is common in military and aerobatic aircraft.

Pros

  • Most important upgrade
  • Improves realism
  • More precise control

Cons

  • Can be expensive
  • Takes desk space

Throttles

A throttle allows realistic engine control and is especially useful for airliners and multi-engine aircraft.

Pros

  • Improves immersion
  • Better power control

Cons

  • Additional cost
  • Extra USB port needed

Rudder Pedals

Rudder pedals control the aircraft's yaw and are most useful during taxiing, takeoff, and landing.

Pros

  • Most realistic setup
  • Better ground handling

Cons

  • Large footprint
  • Usually bought last

Summary

Flight simulation can be enjoyed with just a keyboard, but dedicated hardware greatly improves realism and control. For most beginners, I recommend starting with a yoke and throttle before investing in more advanced equipment such as rudder pedals.
